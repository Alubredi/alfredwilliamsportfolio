# The De-weaponization of Jargon: Lessons from Editing the 145-Page Presidential Report on North-East Rehabilitation

## Executive Key Takeaways

- In statecraft and humanitarian policy, dense bureaucratic jargon is often weaponized to evade accountability and mask institutional inaction.
- Editing a 145-page national security and economic recovery document requires ruthless structural restructuring to ensure donor agencies and field coordinators execute aligned strategies.
- Clear prose is a democratic imperative: if citizens and frontline workers cannot understand a government report, public oversight is impossible.

---

## Investigation Content

In 1946, George Orwell wrote that political language is designed *"to make lies sound truthful and murder respectable, and to give an appearance of solidity to pure wind."* Nowhere is this warning more urgent than in the editing of national post-conflict and humanitarian rehabilitation policies.

      
## The Weight of 145 Pages

      
In 2016, during my tenure at Plug Media Nigeria, I was assigned to edit the definitive policy document of the **Presidential Committee on the North-East Initiative (PCNI)**—a monumental 145-page blueprint commissioned for President Muhammadu Buhari’s administration, focused on counter-terrorism rehabilitation, disarmament, and socio-economic rebuilding in the wake of the Boko Haram insurgency.

      
The manuscript that arrived on my desk was typical of governmental drafting: bloated with passive verbs, euphemisms, circular logic, and jargon: *"operationalization of synergistic socio-infrastructural stabilization paradigms."*

      
> "When an official report uses fifteen words where four will do, ask yourself what harsh truth those extra eleven words are attempting to hide."

      
## The Humanitarian Stakes of Ambiguity

      
This was not an academic exercise. Behind every sterile sentence lay hundreds of thousands of displaced families in Maiduguri, scorched agricultural fields in Adamawa, and malnourished children in refugee camps. If an international donor agency or a military logistics officer could not immediately discern who was responsible for borehole water drilling or grain distribution, human lives were directly at risk.

      
## The Editorial Cure: Direct Subject-Verb-Object Precision

      
Over intense weeks of editorial triage, my mandate was to systematically de-weaponize this jargon:

      
- Transforming passive evasions (*"Deficits were encountered"*) into active accountability (*"The regional ministry failed to deliver 4,000 metric tons of fertilizer"*).
- Eliminating corporate buzzwords and replacing them with measurable, concrete milestones.
- Structuring executive summaries so that ministers and field directors could absorb critical directives within five minutes.

      
Clear prose is not a luxury in public policy; it is the fundamental currency of good governance.
